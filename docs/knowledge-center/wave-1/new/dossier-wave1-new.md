## Claims ledger — water changes

1. **A water change is the primary control for nitrate, which biological
   filtration produces and does not remove.**
   · Carried from `dossier-nitrogen-cycle.md` claims 1–3 · HIGH
   · Uncontested; already published on the hub, so the two agree by construction.
2. **Chlorine and chloramine kill nitrifying bacteria, so replacement water must
   be treated before it enters the tank.**
   · Carried from the tap-water rewrite · HIGH
   · The Iraqi chlorine-vs-chloramine question remains RESEARCH BLOCKED; this
   article does not restate it, it links out.
3. **Large sudden changes shift temperature and chemistry faster than fish
   acclimate; small frequent changes are gentler than one large one.**
   · Standard husbandry, consistent across the sources used elsewhere in Wave 1
   · MEDIUM-HIGH · Published as a principle, with no percentage presented as a
   universal law.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** the "correct" percentage and frequency. Every defensible
answer depends on stocking, feeding, planting and tank volume. The article gives
the *decision rule* — let the nitrate reading set the schedule — instead of a
number dressed as science.

## Claims ledger — quarantine

4. **Most disease enters an established tank on a newly added fish.**
   · Consistent across the white-spot sources (Fishlore, The Aquarium Wiki) and
   standard practice · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · Stated as the reason quarantine exists, not as a measured proportion. No
   percentage is invented.
5. **Ich has a free-swimming stage that must find a host, so a fallow period
   without fish breaks the cycle.**
   · Carried from the white-spot rewrite; UF/IFAS CIR920 · HIGH
   · The mechanism is why quarantine duration is measured in weeks, not days.
6. **Store water can carry pathogens even when the fish looks healthy, so it
   should not be poured into the display tank.**
   · Standard practice · MEDIUM · Framed as a precaution, not a claim about any
   particular seller — the editorial guard forbids implying anything about other
   shops, and there is no reason to.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** an exact quarantine duration. Sources cluster around two
to four weeks but none of the material reviewed gives a controlled basis for a
single figure. Published as a range with the reasoning, so a reader can decide.

## Claims ledger — stocking

7. **The "one inch of fish per gallon" rule is unreliable.**
   · Fishtank Mastery; Aquapacity; Tropical Treasures stocking guides
   · specialist reference · HIGH
   · It ignores body mass, waste output, filtration and behaviour. A 12-inch
   oscar and twelve 1-inch tetras are not comparable loads.
8. **Bioload tracks body mass and metabolism, not length.**
   · Same sources · HIGH · Uncontested among them.
9. **Surface area governs gas exchange, so a shallow wide tank supports more
   fish than a tall narrow one of equal volume.**
   · Geisler (1981), cited by the calculator literature · specialist · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · Attributed as an approach, not published as a formula to compute with.
10. **Filtration capacity and schooling/territorial needs change the answer as
    much as volume does.**
    · Same sources · HIGH

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a general stocking formula. None of the sources endorses
one; the calculators run several methods at once precisely because no single one
holds. The article therefore refuses to give a number and gives the same arbiter
the rest of Wave 1 uses — the ammonia and nitrate readings.
