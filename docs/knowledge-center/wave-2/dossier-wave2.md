## Claims ledger — acclimation

1. **A sealed shipping bag drops in pH as CO2 accumulates, which shifts ammonia
   toward the far less toxic NH4+ form.**
   · Humble.Fish acclimation discussion; Aqua Imports acclimation procedure
   · specialist reference · HIGH
   · Consistent with the EPA pH/NH3 relationship already in the nitrogen dossier.
2. **Opening the bag lets CO2 outgas, pH rises, and the ammonia already in the
   water converts toward toxic NH3 — so a long open-bag drip can poison the fish
   it is meant to protect.**
   · Same sources · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · This is the counterintuitive core of the article. It is a mechanism, not a
   measured rate; published as "the longer the bag stays open, the worse it
   gets" rather than with a timeline.
3. **For shipped fish, temperature-float then transfer without the bag water is
   preferred by several vendors over an extended drip.**
   · Humble.Fish; Tropical Treasures acclimation guide · specialist · MEDIUM
   · **Genuinely contested.** Presented as a reasoned trade-off with the
   mechanism, so the reader can judge, not as a settled rule.
4. **Bag water should never enter the display tank.**
   · Universal across sources · HIGH · Also the quarantine article's position.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** how long is too long. No source gives a defensible
threshold, and it depends on how long the fish was bagged, the ammonia already
present, and the pH gap. The article gives the mechanism and the direction.

## Claims ledger — test-kit interpretation

5. **Ammonia and nitrite must read zero in an established tank; nitrate is
   tolerated and controlled by water changes.**
   · Carried from `dossier-nitrogen-cycle.md` · HIGH
6. **The same ammonia number is far more dangerous at high pH, because the
   toxic NH3 fraction rises with pH and temperature.**
   · EPA 440/5-85-001 via the nitrogen dossier, claims 4-5 · HIGH
   · Already published on the hub; the two agree by construction.
7. **Nitrite blocks oxygen transport, so nitrite poisoning looks like
   suffocation even in clear, well-aerated water.**
   · Texas A&M AgriLife extension, via the nitrogen dossier claim 7 · HIGH

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a numeric "safe" nitrate ceiling. Commonly repeated
figures were not traced to a primary source within this dossier, so the article
gives the *trend* rule — stable between water changes is fine, climbing is not —
which is the same handling Wave 1 used for stocking and water changes.

## Claims ledger — GH / KH / TDS

8. **KH is carbonate hardness and acts as the pH buffer; GH is general hardness,
   mostly calcium and magnesium; TDS is total dissolved solids and is not a
   substitute for either.**
   · Standard water chemistry, consistent across the aquarium references used
   throughout Wave 1 · HIGH
9. **Low KH makes a pH crash likely, and nitrification itself consumes
   alkalinity — so a cycling tank with low KH can crash mid-cycle.**
   · `dossier-nitrogen-cycle.md` claim 9 (Wikipedia fishless cycling; Puffer
   Forum library) · MEDIUM
   · That dossier explicitly limits this to "slows sharply and can stall", not a
   hard cutoff. Published with the same hedge and the same ~3 dKH figure framed
   as a rough threshold.
10. **Nitrification slows sharply as pH falls and effectively stalls near 6.0.**
    · Same source · MEDIUM · Same hedge.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** Iraqi tap water GH/KH values. The truth contract forbids
generalising Iraqi water chemistry, and no source was found. The article tells
the reader to measure, exactly as the tap-water and nitrogen articles do.

## Claims ledger — aquarium salt

11. **Salt is a medication, not a supplement; freshwater fish do not require
    routine salt.**
    · Consistent across the sources reviewed · MEDIUM-HIGH
12. **Whether scaleless fish and corydoras tolerate salt is genuinely disputed.**
    · Against: fish-vet positions cited on AquariaCentral. For: PlanetCatfish
    keeper reports, and an IAAAM 1995 study reporting 16 of 16 *Corydoras
    aeneus* surviving 1.0 ppt · **CONTESTED**
    · **Published as a disagreement, not resolved.** The safe reading — be
    cautious with scaleless species and dose deliberately — follows from the
    disagreement itself and does not require picking a winner.
13. **Salt does not evaporate, so it only leaves in a water change. "Topping up"
    salt after evaporation concentrates it.**
    · Basic chemistry, uncontested · HIGH
    · The most actionable safety point in the article.
14. **Plants are generally sensitive to salt at therapeutic doses.**
    · Consistent across sources · MEDIUM

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a dose that reliably cures ich. Doses vary by source and
species and the parasite's exposed stage is brief; the white-spot article's
temperature-plus-medication protocol is the one this corpus stands behind. No
dose is published here.
