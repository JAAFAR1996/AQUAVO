## Cannibalisation resolved by reframing, not by adding

The largest unowned topic was oxygen: 21 articles mention aeration, none owns
it. But `air-pumps-decoration-or-necessity` sits right next to it, so publishing
a separate oxygen article would have split one topic across two pages — the same
problem Wave 1 spent its time undoing.

So the air-pump page was **widened to own the parameter**, with the pump as one
answer inside it, keeping its URL. This is the opposite move from a merge and it
solves the same problem: one topic, one page.

## Claims ledger — dissolved oxygen

1. **Gas exchange occurs at any air-water interface, including each bubble's,
   and an air stone also drives circulation that renews the surface.**
   · Aquarium Science 9.1; Advanced Aquarium Concepts; hobby forum debate
   · specialist reference · **CONTESTED IN THE HOBBY, not in the physics**
   · The popular line — bubbles do not oxygenate, only surface agitation does —
   is an oversimplification. Published as both mechanisms operating, with the
   relative contribution depending on tank geometry: tall narrow tanks lean on
   the bubbles, shallow wide tanks on the surface. The practical advice is
   identical either way, so accuracy costs nothing here.
2. **Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen while fish demand more: about
   9.1 mg/L at 20 °C against about 7.0 at 35 °C, with metabolism roughly
   doubling per 10 °C.**
   · Carried from `dossier-summer-heat.md` claims 1-3 · HIGH
3. **Nitrification consumes oxygen, so the filter is itself a consumer.**
   · Carried from `dossier-nitrogen-cycle.md` · HIGH
4. **Nitrite poisoning presents like suffocation, because it blocks oxygen
   transport rather than removing oxygen from the water.**
   · Carried from the nitrogen dossier claim 7 · HIGH
   · This is why the article tells the reader to test before assuming the
   problem is aeration.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a target dissolved-oxygen figure for a home tank. Hobby DO
test kits are uncommon and thresholds are species-dependent. The article gives
the behavioural signal instead — surface gasping, worst before dawn — which is
what a keeper can actually observe.

## Claims ledger — symptom diagnosis

5. **Most sudden "disease" in a beginner tank is water poisoning presenting with
   disease-like symptoms.**
   · Consistent across the sources used throughout Waves 1-2 · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · Published as an ordering rule — test first — rather than as a proportion.
   No percentage is invented.
6. **Fin rot usually follows poor water quality or stress, so treating the
   infection without fixing the water lets it return.**
   · Standard husbandry, consistent with the existing fin-rot article · HIGH
7. **Preventive medication without a diagnosis stresses fish and harms the
   filter's bacteria.**
   · Consistent with the white-spot and human-medicine articles · MEDIUM-HIGH

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** definitive diagnosis from appearance alone. Many symptoms
are shared between causes. The article says so in its closing line and positions
the table as a ranking of likelihoods, not a diagnosis.
