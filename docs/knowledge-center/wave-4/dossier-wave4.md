## Claims ledger — invertebrates

Format: CLAIM · SOURCE · TYPE · CONFIDENCE · LIMITATION

1. **Invertebrates are far more sensitive to copper than fish; the target for a
   shrimp or snail tank is undetectable copper.**
   · Flip Aquatics copper guide; Aquarium Breeder; Aquatic Community
   · specialist reference · HIGH
   · Uncontested across the sources reviewed.
2. **Many fish medications and almost all snail treatments are copper-based, and
   will kill shrimp at doses that do not harm fish.**
   · Same sources · HIGH
   · This is the article's headline, and it connects directly to the white-spot
   and diagnosis articles: treat in a separate tank.
3. **Copper does not break down; it binds to substrate, rock and silicone and
   leaches back for months, so a treated tank may never be invertebrate-safe.**
   · Aquarium Advice; Peqaboo invertebrate guide · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · "May never be" is the sources' own hedge and is published as such.
4. **Trace copper is required nutritionally and is present in normal foods.**
   · Aquarium Breeder · MEDIUM · Included so the article does not read as
   "copper is poison", which would be wrong.
5. **Shrimp and snails need adequate calcium for shell and exoskeleton; very
   soft water causes moulting and shell problems.**
   · Consistent across the sources · MEDIUM-HIGH · Ties to the GH article.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a safe copper ppm for a mixed tank. Sources say zero for
invertebrates and give varying therapeutic numbers for fish. The article gives
the rule — never dose copper into a tank meant for invertebrates — rather than a
figure that would invite someone to aim at it.

## Claims ledger — schooling

6. **Shoaling species kept below their group size show chronic stress:
   hiding, faded colour, reduced feeding, and intraspecies aggression.**
   · Standard husbandry, consistent across the species references used in Waves
   1-3 · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · Published as a behavioural description, which is what a keeper can verify.
7. **Chronic stress suppresses immunity, which is why undersized groups get sick
   repeatedly with no other cause.**
   · Consistent with the white-spot sources on stress as a trigger · MEDIUM-HIGH
8. **Six of the same species is the practical floor for true shoalers; six mixed
   individuals do not form a shoal.**
   · Widely repeated across specialist keeping guides · MEDIUM
   · A convention rather than a measured threshold. Published as "the practical
   minimum", not as a biological constant.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a species-by-species minimum. Numbers vary by source and
by tank. The article gives four behavioural patterns instead of a species list
it cannot support.

## Claims ledger — travel

9. **Healthy adult fish tolerate several days without food; deteriorating water
   is the greater risk during an absence.**
   · Consistent across general husbandry sources · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · Published with the explicit exceptions: fry, sick fish, fish in treatment.
10. **Overfeeding before or during an absence is the common cause of loss, since
    uneaten food decomposes and raises ammonia with nobody watching.**
    · Consistent with the nitrogen dossier · HIGH

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a maximum safe fasting period. It depends on species, age,
size and tank stability. The article gives a graded table by absence length and
names the exceptions rather than a single number.

## Claims ledger — sexing

11. **Anal fin shape is the most reliable visual sex difference in livebearers:
    a pointed gonopodium in males, a fan-shaped fin in females.**
    · Standard across livebearer references · HIGH
    · The one genuinely dependable marker, and the article says so.
12. **Two to three females per male reduces harassment in livebearers.**
    · Widely repeated husbandry convention · MEDIUM · Published as a practical
    ratio, not a rule.
13. **Many commonly kept species — most tetras, rasboras and danios — cannot be
    reliably sexed by eye, and juveniles cannot be sexed at all.**
    · Consistent across sources · HIGH
    · Stated plainly. An article that implied otherwise would be worse than none.
