## Claims ledger — feeding

1. **Uneaten food decomposes, raising ammonia and feeding both bacterial blooms
   and algae.** · Carried from the nitrogen and cloudy-water dossiers · HIGH
2. **The practical measure of "enough" is what is consumed in about two
   minutes.** · Standard husbandry convention · MEDIUM
   · A convention, not a measurement. Published as the practical rule it is.
3. **Ingredient order indicates feed quality; fish or shrimp meal first is better
   than wheat or grain fillers.** · Carried from the existing tetra-food article
   · MEDIUM
4. **Vitamins in prepared food degrade over time and faster in heat.**
   · Consistent across feed references · MEDIUM · Relevant to Iraqi storage.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a universal feeding frequency. It varies with species, age,
temperature and tank maturity. The article gives a table by *situation* and the
two-minute rule rather than one number.

## Claims ledger — fungus vs columnaris

5. **Columnaris is bacterial (*Flavobacterium columnare*), not fungal, despite
   the "cotton wool disease" nickname, and does not respond to antifungals.**
   · Bassleer; ProHobby columnaris guide; American Aquarium Products
   · specialist reference · HIGH
   · The single most valuable correction in this wave: the wasted antifungal
   window is what kills, not treatment difficulty.
6. **True aquarium "fungus" (Saprolegnia) is an oomycete water mould, not a true
   fungus, and grows on wounds or dead tissue.**
   · Aquarium Science 10.7; Bassleer · specialist reference · HIGH
7. **Visual discriminator: three-dimensional fluffy growth at a wound suggests
   Saprolegnia; a flat patch attached to the body away from any wound suggests
   columnaris.** · Bassleer; ProHobby · MEDIUM-HIGH
   · Published as a *ranking*, with an explicit statement that definitive
   identification needs microscopy.
8. **Both are opportunistic — they follow injury, poor water or stress.**
   · Consistent across sources · HIGH

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** a treatment protocol naming specific antibiotics. Product
availability and regulation differ by country and the corpus does not stock or
recommend antibiotics. The article routes to dose-on-the-label products AQUAVO
actually carries, and says raising temperature — correct for ich — may
*accelerate* columnaris, which is the dangerous cross-protocol mistake.

## Claims ledger — airborne toxins

9. **An air pump draws room air and injects it into the water, so airborne
   contaminants reach the tank by the most direct possible route.**
   · Mechanically self-evident from how an air pump works · HIGH
   · This is the article's organising insight and explains sudden mass mortality
   with clean water tests.
10. **Insecticide aerosols are designed to kill arthropods, so invertebrates are
    the most exposed inhabitants.** · Consistent with the Wave 4 copper research
    on invertebrate sensitivity · MEDIUM-HIGH
11. **Soap, hand cream and alcohol sanitiser enter directly on hands.**
    · Standard husbandry caution · HIGH

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** how long to keep an air pump off during spraying, and how
much of a given aerosol reaches the water. No source quantifies it. The article
gives the trade-off instead — long enough to avoid the spray, short enough not
to create an oxygen problem — and links to the oxygen article.

## Claims ledger — jumping

12. **Jumping is natural behaviour (predator escape, moving between waters,
    catching insects), so its presence is not pathological but its repetition is
    a signal.** · Standard behavioural description · MEDIUM-HIGH
13. **Common triggers are poor water, low oxygen, harassment and heat.**
    · Consistent with the diagnosis and oxygen articles · MEDIUM-HIGH
14. **A fully sealed lid is dangerous for obligate air-breathers, which must
    reach the surface.** · Carried from the no-filter article, claim 7
    (*Journal of Morphology*) · HIGH
    · This is why the article argues for covering gaps rather than sealing.

**RESEARCH BLOCKED:** survival odds for a fish found out of water. It depends on
elapsed time and gill drying, and no source gives usable numbers. The article
says recovery is possible if the fish is still moist and recently out, and that
it may not survive regardless.
